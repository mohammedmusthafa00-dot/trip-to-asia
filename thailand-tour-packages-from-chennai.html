Why travellers from Chennai choose Thailand

Thailand is one of the easiest international trips from Chennai: the flight to Bangkok takes around four hours, the food is familiar, and a Tamil-speaking guide makes sightseeing far more enjoyable for families and older travellers.

Our Chennai travellers usually ask for three things: Indian meals, hotels that are comfortable without being expensive, and a guide who can explain temples and history in Tamil. Every Trip to Asia package includes a 3-star hotel stay, airport transfers, entry fees and Indian food (3 meals), and tours with a Tamil guide run on the packages where listed (minimum 4 members).

Time difference and weather

Thailand is 1 hour 30 minutes ahead of India, so there is hardly any jet lag. The cool and dry season from November to February is the most comfortable time to visit.

Flights and planning from Chennai

Chennai has direct flights to Bangkok on several airlines, so most trips begin with a non-stop flight of around four hours. Schedules and fares change often, so send us your preferred dates and ask about flights when you enquire. We will fit the packages around them.

You can also send a custom itinerary request with your own destinations and dates, or read our guides to Bangkok, Pattaya and Phuket.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a Tamil-speaking guide on Thailand tours from Chennai?

Yes. City and island tours with a Tamil guide are included in the packages where listed, with a minimum of 4 members.

How many days should I plan for a first trip from Chennai?

Most first-time visitors choose Bangkok + Pattaya (4 nights / 5 days) or Bangkok Escape (3 nights / 4 days).

What is the best time to visit Thailand from Chennai?

November to February is cool and dry across most of Thailand. The Andaman coast (Phuket and Krabi) is at its best from about November to April.

Can my whole family travel together?

Yes. Send your destinations, dates and number of travellers (up to 10) on the Custom Itinerary page and we will plan it around your group.
